About this Atlas

This interactive atlas traces the rise, peak, and decline of major pre-colonial African states and empires between 500 and 1850 CE. Each node marks a political or cultural center — not a fixed border. Boundaries shifted constantly, and many polities overlapped in time and space.

The data draws on the UNESCO General History of Africa (volumes II–VI), peer-reviewed archaeological excavations at sites such as Aksum, Meroë, Great Zimbabwe, Jenne-jeno, Gao, Timbuktu, Ife, Benin City, and Mbanza Kongo, and primary textual accounts where they survive — including Ibn Battuta, al-Bakri, Leo Africanus, and the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea.

Move the timeline slider to see which states were active in any given year. Markers swell and brighten during a state's peak; shaded polygons show each major empire's approximate peak territorial extent. Trade routes appear only when active. A note on evidence: not every state left a written record. Many are known primarily through archaeology or oral tradition — this is reflected in each entry's evidence type.
